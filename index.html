What it does — in thirty seconds

It reads the entire report the way your most meticulous reviewer would — every sentence, every table, every figure — and writes what it finds as ordinary Word comments, anchored to the exact spot they concern.

Spelling & terminology — British English, the correct BREEAM terms, the firm's own wording.

Formatting & structure — captions, headings, leftover template text.

Numbers & consistency — figures that contradict each other, claims the evidence doesn't support.

Images & maps — the visuals, checked against what the text says about them.

Completeness — required evidence and annexes actually present, scheme and version matching.

The firm's rulebook — every calibrated Sureal rule, checked on every run, provably.

You still judge, decide, and sign — that never changes. What changes is where you start: not at page one hunting typos and cross-checking numbers, but at the judgment only an expert can make.

1 · Quickstart — your two jobs

You do two things with this app. Everything else in this manual is background.

Job 1 · every report

Get a review

Upload a report; it comes back with AI comments anchored in the document, ready to work through in Word.

~2 min of your time · 5–20 min for the system Job 2 · once a month

Teach the AI

Reply in Word to comments you disagree with, then drop the finished file into the shared folder. That's the entire hand-over.

reply only where it's wrong · monthly

Run a report — five steps

  1. Open the app via the Sureal Report Reviewer icon on your desktop. It starts the app if needed and opens it in your browser. You're in when the green Sureal Report Reviewer page loads.
  2. Check the Catalogue — the Catalogue page in the app shows every credit and how far along it is. This manual deliberately never lists them: the list grows every month, and the Catalogue is always current. Three levels:

    Calibrated — the full review, including Sureal's own expert rules. Treat it like a careful junior colleague's pass.

    Tested — proven on real reports: the mechanical checks and AI audits run and catch real issues, but the firm-specific rulebook isn't loaded yet.

    Not yet tested — it runs, but treat the output as an experiment, and tell Sureal what you see.

  3. Upload. Choose the report (.docx), select the scheme (NC2016 / RFO2015 / V6 / V7), credit and stage, and confirm the building name. (About a minute.)
  4. Wait — 5 to 20 minutes depending on document size. The status page shows each phase live; you can close the tab and come back.
  5. Download. The file keeps its original name — save it over your working copy, or next to it. You're done when the document opens in Word with comments in the margin.

Reading the output — one minute to learn

Every comment the system writes has the same four parts:

Sureal Report Reviewer
[TERM.2] LOW — Terminology

The document uses “dismountable”; the BREEAM term is “demountable”.

Action Required: replace “dismountable” with “demountable” throughout section 3.2.

Source: rule TERM.2 · calibrated

Rule + severity → what's wrong, in one line → Action Required tells you exactly what to change → Source names the rule that produced it.

SeverityWhat to do
CRITICALCompliance-blocking: mandatory BREEAM evidence is missing or a core claim is unsupported. Fix before the report goes anywhere — rare by design; more than a couple means the document has a real problem.
HIGHA likely assessor finding: evidence gaps, contradictions between sections, unsubstantiated claims. Fix before submission.
MEDIUMWeakens the evidence or the document's consistency: vague commitments, template text, inconsistent KPIs. Fix unless you have a documented reason not to.
LOWPolish: terminology (e.g. BREEAM's "demountable"), British English, captions, formatting. Seconds each, fix in the same pass.

The review is honest about itself. An uncalibrated credit says so in its output. If one of the audit passes couldn't run, the result says “complete — N of M audits unavailable” with the reason. What you see is exactly what was checked — and what wasn't.

Proof of what was checked. On the results page, next to the download for your reviewed document, there is a second, smaller download: the coverage report (a file called rule_coverage.json). Open it and you'll see one entry for every rule in the rulebook — each one either points to the comment it produced in your document, or says "checked — clean" with a short reason. No rule is ever skipped or sampled. When a client or assessor asks how thoroughly a report was reviewed, this file is the answer — hand it over as it is.

If the document states a different BREEAM version than the scheme you selected, the review flags it (SCH.1). That flag is usually right — check the project's actual registration before dismissing it.

Reply to the comments — this is where the teaching happens

Your replies in Word are how the system learns. Three replies carry meaning:

Agreeagreed, will fix

Confirms the rule is earning its place.

Disagree + whydisagree — this is in annex 3

The most valuable reply you can write. Recurring disagreement flags the rule for expert review and retunes it.

Suppressstop flagging this

Tells the system this check isn't wanted; enough of these and the rule is retired.

Silence counts weakly as agreement — a wrong comment left unanswered stays wrong next month. Two lines of reply fixes next month's reviews, not just this document.

The one golden rule: keep working in the reviewed file. Your replies only exist in the document that holds them — copy the text into a fresh file and the teaching dies with the old one.

2 · Providing feedback to the AI

Your replies only teach the system if they reach it. The channel is one shared folder — no forms, no mails, no exports.

How it works

Your admin has connected the app to a shared calibration folder in your organisation's OneDrive/SharePoint. Everything dropped there is collected once a month, turned into verdicts, reviewed rule by rule by a human expert, and published to the whole team as an update. Nothing changes the AI automatically — a person approves every rule. And your own feedback comes back as behaviour: the comment you corrected this month stops appearing next month.

One-time check — is the folder connected? (30 seconds)

Open File Explorer → your organisation's OneDrive/SharePoint area → you should see the Calibration folder with a green sync check. If it isn't there, or its files show a cloud icon instead of a check, ask your admin — there is nothing for you to configure.

Handing over a document — the entire “upload”

  1. Finish your replies in Word, save, and close the file.
  2. Drop a copy into the calibration folder. That's it — the folder is the upload.
  3. Leave it alone: no renaming, no moving it out, one copy per report per month. The monthly round pairs each file against the system's own records — renaming breaks the pairing. You're done when the file shows the green sync check.

What happens with it

At month-end, the system turns the month's replies and disputed comments into a short verdict workbook; your experts mark agree / disagree / suppress — a few minutes, once a month. The admin then approves or rejects every proposal individually, and the result reaches everyone through the ordinary update banner. Your two-line reply in March is a better review in April.

Also feeding this folder: candidates files from teaching a new credit its rules — see §6.

3 · If a report fails

Runs almost never fail — but when one does, you do exactly one thing: send a screenshot. There is nothing to troubleshoot on your side, ever.

First — has it actually failed?

Still working — do nothing

Slow is normal

5–20 minutes is a normal run, and the status page shows a live heartbeat while phases complete. As long as phases keep ticking, walk away and come back — a slow run is not a failed run. Also keep in mind, the speed will improve over time.

Failed — send the screenshot

The page tells you

A failed run shows a red error panel on the status page, or says “possibly stalled” and stays that way after a refresh. That page is the error log — which is exactly why the fix is a screenshot.

The three steps

  1. Screenshot the status page — the whole browser window, so the error panel and the phase list are both visible. Press Win + Shift + S, drag across the window, done. That one image tells Sureal everything: which phase, which error, when.
  2. Email it to Ruben with one line of context — which credit, and roughly when you started the run.
    ✉ Mail the screenshot → ruben.geudens@sureal.be
  3. That's it. Leave the tab open if you can, and carry on with other work — you don't need to retry, refresh, or reinstall anything. You're done when the mail is sent. The fix happens on Sureal's side.

Why runs fail — the two usual suspects

💳

The AI usage budget ran out. Reviews run on metered AI credits (a usage budget — nothing to do with BREEAM credits). When the budget is exhausted, runs fail until it's topped up — a same-day fix on Sureal's side, and the most common cause by far.

⛈

The AI provider is overloaded or down. Rare and always temporary. Nothing to fix anywhere — the run simply works again later. Your screenshot still helps: it tells Sureal whether anything else is going on.

Neither cause is your document, and neither is your machine. If a run fails for any other reason, the screenshot is precisely what lets Sureal find and fix it — often before you've finished your coffee.

Your document is always safe. The system works on a copy of your upload — a failed run never touches or damages your file. Re-running the same report after Ruben gives the all-clear is always safe.

4 · The first month — what to expect

A small ask before you start: give it a month of patience — and your corrections. This system cannot become perfect on its own; it becomes perfect through your expert feedback. That's not a disclaimer, it's the design.

It arrives knowing BREEAM and your historical review rules — it hasn't yet met your judgment on live work. So in the first weeks, expect the occasional comment that misses context, fires too eagerly, or isn't worded the way you'd say it. Each one needs the same thing: a two-line reply in Word. That's the entire ask.

The deal, plainly. A review can carry 30-odd comments — you don't reply to them all. Most are simply right: fix and move on. Your part is a two-line reply on each comment that's wrong, plus one month of tolerance for rough edges. Every monthly calibration turns those replies into expert-approved rule changes — and a comment you correct once disappears for the whole team, permanently.
Month 1rough edges + your replies
Calibrationexpert approves each fix
Month 2disputed comments gone
Every month aftersharper, in your voice

Improvement is targeted, not hopeful — calibration works from your actual verdicts. And it never gets worse silently: changes only arrive as versioned, tested releases.

5 · Quick install — up and running in 15 minutes

Two short jobs, each done once: install the app (~10 minutes, the installer does the work), then connect the shared feedback folder (~2 minutes). You only ever do one thing at a time: copy a line, paste it, press Enter.

Part 1 · Install the app — once, ~10 minutes

Before you start
  • A Windows 10/11 PC — no admin rights needed
  • The installer file from Sureal's most recent mail, saved into your Downloads folder (it's personal to your organisation — don't forward it)
  • The API key Sureal sent you through a separate channel — keep it where you can retype it
  1. Open PowerShell — press Start, type powershell, click Windows PowerShell. A blue window opens. (The normal one — never "Run as administrator".)
  2. Paste this line and press Enter. It finds the installer in Downloads and starts it: 
    cd $HOME\Downloads; $z = Get-ChildItem install-win*.zip -ErrorAction SilentlyContinue | Sort-Object LastWriteTime -Descending | Select-Object -First 1; if ($z) { Expand-Archive $z.FullName -DestinationPath . -Force }; $s = Get-ChildItem -Recurse -Filter install-win.ps1 -ErrorAction SilentlyContinue | Sort-Object LastWriteTime -Descending | Select-Object -First 1; if (-not $s) { Write-Host "[ERROR] installer not found - save the file from Sureal's mail into Downloads first" -ForegroundColor Red } else { powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File $s.FullName }
    If it prints a red installer not found, the file isn't in Downloads yet — save it from the mail and paste the same line again.
  3. Watch it work. The installer runs nine numbered steps (finding Python ✓, finding git ✓, downloading the software ✓ …). You don't do anything until it asks.
    Step 1 of 9

    If it stops saying Python is missing: open the Microsoft Store (Start → type "store"), search for Python, click Get on the newest version, wait for it to finish — then paste the install line again. No admin rights needed, and re-running is always safe.

    Step 5 of 9

    Sits still for 1–3 minutes while it builds the environment. It is not stuck — perfect coffee moment.

    Step 7 of 9

    It asks for the API key. Type it yourself — typing works better than pasting on this prompt — and press Enter. The characters stay hidden while you type: that's a security feature, not a bug.

    Step 8 of 9

    A free self-test runs (no AI cost). It reviews a built-in document to prove everything works before you ever touch a real one.

    See a yellow WARNING at the self-test? The app still works — keep going, and send the yellow lines to your admin so Sureal can have a look. Only a hard stop needs attention: screenshot it and send it to your admin.
  4. Done — the app opens by itself. Your browser shows the green Sureal Report Reviewer page, and a Sureal Report Reviewer icon is on your desktop. From now on, that icon is how you open the app — it starts everything and opens your browser at http://127.0.0.1:8000. You're done when you see the green page and the desktop icon exists.
Three things that keep this stress-free. Re-running the installer is always safe — it never damages an existing install, so "paste the line again" is the universal fix. In PowerShell, silence means it worked. And everything runs on your own machine — documents only leave it for the AI review calls.

Network error during install? Ask Sureal for the offline dependency bundle and re-run with that folder beside the installer. A message about your Python version isn't a network problem — send Sureal that exact line.

Part 2 · Connect the calibration folder — once, ~2 minutes

The calibration folder is the shared OneDrive/SharePoint folder that carries your feedback to Sureal automatically — no downloads, no mails. Your admin has already created it; your job is only to connect to it.

  1. Accept the shared folder — open the invitation your admin sent (or the SharePoint library link) and click Sync. Let OneDrive finish syncing; the folder appears in File Explorer under your organisation's name. (No invitation? Ask your admin.)
  2. Keep it on your PC: in File Explorer, right-click the calibration folder → "Always keep on this device" — so the app reads real files, not cloud placeholders.
  3. Let the app find it: open the app → Calibration page → click "Search this machine". The app recognises the folder by a hidden marker inside it and connects on its own. You're done when the Calibration page shows the folder as connected.

If "Search this machine" doesn't find it: check the sync actually finished, or paste the folder's local path (the one starting with C:\ — never the https://… SharePoint link, and without quotation marks around it) and click Connect. Still stuck? That's an admin question, not a you-problem.

Not connected yet? Nothing is lost. The app works fully without the folder — anything you capture stays on your machine, and a green download button on the results screen hands it over instead. Connect whenever convenient.

Updating — one click, whenever the banner appears

When a new version exists, a banner appears in the app: "Version X available." Open the Update page and click once. The updater is transactional — it refuses while a review is running, backs everything up, applies the update, re-runs the self-test, and rolls back automatically if anything fails — and it has been proven in the field, not just in theory. Your local data is never touched.

🛟

Any trouble with either part? Don't troubleshoot — take a screenshot and ask your admin. They'll sort it out with Sureal, usually the same day.

6 · The admin guide — one seat that governs the learning

One install per organisation is the admin seat. It doesn't review reports differently — it decides what the system is allowed to learn. Setup is once (~15 minutes plus sync time); the routine is monthly.

The mental model — three roles, one direction of travel

Every reviewer

Captures. Replies on AI comments and uploaded reviewed reports become proposals — nothing more.

The admin (you)

Creates and reviews. You set up the shared calibration folder once — every seat then finds it — and you review the month's queue.

Sureal

Approves and releases. Every rule is validated and approved individually, then reaches the whole team as a versioned update.

The one guarantee behind it all: nothing anyone does — reviewer, admin, or Sureal — ever changes a live rulebook directly. Everything travels through staging, a human decision, and a versioned, reversible release. On every non-admin install, the admin page simply doesn't exist: not hidden behind a login, absent by design.

One-time setup · Step 1 — activate the admin page

Install the app first like any seat (§5, Part 1). Then, on your machine only, open PowerShell (normal, not "as administrator") and paste:

cd "$env:LOCALAPPDATA\Sureal\breeam-pipeline"; & ".\.venv\Scripts\python.exe" -c "from calibration import admin; admin.set_admin(True); print('is_admin:', admin.is_admin())"

This is deliberately not settable from the web interface — an admin seat is created at the keyboard, not clicked into existence.

You'll see is_admin: True and nothing else. Reload the app — a Rules admin entry appears in the top bar.

To hand the seat over or step down, run the same line with False — the page returns "not found" again, which is the correct state for every other install.

Step 2 — store the write token

Sureal sends you the write token through a secure channel (Teams or SMS — never in the same mail as anything else). It is the only read-write credential in the whole fleet, which is precisely what makes your seat the only one that can publish.

  1. Open Rules admin → paste the token into the Write token box → save. Once. Nowhere else, ever.
  2. After saving, the app shows a short fingerprint — the only form in which the token is ever displayed, and how you verify which one is stored.
  3. The token expires after one year: put the renewal date in your calendar today. An expired token surfaces as a refused publish, not a warning.
One iron rule: the token never travels through mail, chat, or a terminal. If it ever appears in one — including by accident — tell Sureal immediately; it is revoked and reissued the same day. That costs nothing. A leaked token that stays quiet is the only expensive version.

Step 3 — create the calibration folder (once, for everyone)

The folder is the team's entire hand-over channel: reviewed documents and candidates files go in, your review queue fills itself from it. You create it once; every other seat merely connects (§5, Part 2).

  1. Create a folder in the organisation's shared SharePoint/OneDrive library and sync that library to your machine (SharePoint web → the library → Sync). Let it finish — the folder appears in File Explorer.
  2. Right-click the folder → "Always keep on this device" — the app refuses cloud-only placeholder content.
  3. App → Calibration page → "Set up the folder (Sureal admin, once)" → paste the folder's local path (the one starting with C:\, without quotation marks — never the https://… SharePoint link) → Set up this folder.
  4. Setup verifies the folder is writable, sync-backed and fully on disk, then writes a hidden marker file inside it and creates the working subfolders (inbox, expert-review, processed, quarantine). You're done when the Calibration page shows the folder as set up.

The marker is the folder's identity, not the path. Every seat recognises the folder by the marker inside it, so moving or renaming the folder later is safe — the app re-finds it. The only fatal act is deleting the marker file; don't.

Then tell the team the one rule that matters: drop files in and leave them alone — no renaming, no moving out. And point everyone at §5 to connect their own seat.

The monthly routine — four moves

Ingestthe folder fills your queue
Experts verdictthe review sheet, minutes
You decideapprove / edit / reject, each item
Publishone transaction to the fleet
  1. Ingest. Captured rules and feedback verdicts arrive via the folder; "Check the folder now" (or the background sweep) pulls them into Waiting for review.
  2. Expert verdicts. Export the Expert review sheet per credit — one row per disputed comment; your seniors mark agree / disagree / suppress in minutes. The returning sheet is read back through the same ingest, and only its verdict and comment columns are ever applied; any other change to the sheet is surfaced as a proposal, never silently absorbed.
  3. Decide. Work the queue one item at a time: Approve as proposed / Approve edited / Reject, with an optional "why" kept alongside the decision. Approving changes no rulebook by itself — it only stages.
  4. Publish. One click applies the entire approved set as a single transaction — validation gate, version bump, release — and the team receives it through the ordinary update banner. Publish refuses, loudly and safely, while a review is running, while nothing is approved, while your install is behind the latest release, or while program files have local edits. A refusal is the system protecting the fleet, not a malfunction.

Known issue, current version: on Windows, Publish can refuse with "local edits outside the data paths" naming four pack folders — a false positive of the file-link handling, not real damage. Until the fix ships: decide everything as normal, then tell Sureal a release is ready and Sureal publishes it. This note disappears when the fix lands.

Teaching a credit module its rules

Each credit has its own rule pack, and packs start as scaffolds: the pipeline runs and the mechanical checks work, but the credit-specific rulebook is empty. Any report can test a credit today — upload one document and the mechanical checks and AI audits run immediately, with the rulebook layer reporting itself as uncalibrated. Calibrating — teaching the credit the firm's own rules — needs reviewed history: 8–10 fully reviewed reports (.docx with the reviewers' comments still in them, same credit and stage; a useful document carries at least 6 substantive comments — cover pages and sign-offs teach nothing). Below that, the pack still works but is flagged low-confidence and its findings deserve extra scepticism.

  1. Collect the reviewed DOCX files. Do not rename anything to *_ORIGINAL.docx — that suffix is reserved by the system.
  2. App → Expert Rules → select the credit and stage → upload the documents. Any seat can do this — it doesn't need the admin machine.
  3. The system clusters recurring reviewer concerns into candidate rules behind safety gates: each candidate needs a correct and an incorrect example and must appear in at least two documents. Singletons are parked, not published.
  4. Candidates land in staging — never the live rulebook — and travel to Sureal via the calibration folder (or the green download button on a seat that isn't connected). Approved rules arrive in a future update, and from then on every review of that credit applies them, with the coverage ledger proving it.
What being admin actually means. You are the quality gate between "a reviewer disagreed" and "the whole team's reviews changed". Reject freely — a rejected proposal costs nothing and can always come back with better evidence. The one thing the role never requires is speed: an unpublished queue is a safe queue.

⚠ Admin pitfalls — each learned the hard way

  • Never edit rule database files by hand. A hand-calibrated ruleset was once overwritten by a well-intentioned bulk operation and survived only because of version control. The staged approve flow validates before every write — it exists so this can never happen again.
  • Never delete the marker file in the calibration folder — it is how every machine recognises the folder.
  • One document, one ingest per round. The same report processed twice inflates rule frequencies and can push weak rules over the acceptance threshold.
  • A refused Publish is information, not an obstacle. Read the reason it gives; it names exactly what to resolve.

Anything odd at any step — a setup check that fails, a publish refusal you can't place, a queue that looks wrong — contact Sureal with a screenshot. The admin seat has exactly one escalation path, and it's fast.

7 · The big picture

We have entered the era where software improves itself. This is what that looks like for BREEAM review.

Traditional software is finished the day it ships, and ages from that moment. This system is the opposite: it is an agentic micro-engine — a growing body of firm-owned knowledge (calibrated rules, reviewer verdicts, regulation packs, coverage ledgers) that every review adds to, and that AI agents read, apply, and extend. The software your team uses next quarter will be measurably better than today's — not because a vendor shipped an update, but because your own reviewed reports taught it. Improvement is the product's default state; humans approve every step of it.

The concrete problem it solves today: Sureal reviews BREEAM credit reports with a four-pairs-of-eyes principle — every draft is read, in full, by multiple senior reviewers before it reaches a client. That produces excellent reviews, and consumes senior hours on every report, on checks that repeat from document to document: terminology, consistency, completeness, the firm's hard-won rules about what assessors accept.

This system captures those repeating checks — distilled from hundreds of historical reviewer comments into a calibrated, firm-owned rulebook — and applies all of them, every time, provably, writing its findings as ordinary Word comments into the document itself. The senior eyes then do what only senior eyes can: judge, decide, and sign.

The product is not any single credit review. The product is the infrastructure plus the rulebook — a system Sureal's team extends themselves (new credits, new rules, new schemes) and that compounds: every reviewed report can teach the next one.

8 · The current solution — and why it's the right one

A reviewed document goes through a pipeline of thirteen steps. The principle: deterministic code where determinism belongs, AI where judgment belongs, and a ledger proving nothing was skipped — with failures reported just as loudly as findings: if an audit cannot be delivered, the review says so on its face instead of pretending completeness. Trust is symmetric.

Extracttext, tables, figuresCODE
Mechanical checksformatting · names · scheme matchCODE
Audit wavenumbers · quality · visuals · annexes — in parallelAI
Rule sweepevery calibrated rule: finding or checked-cleanAI
Consolidatemerge · dedupe · prioritiseAI
Word commentsanchored in the documentCODE
Verify + ledgercoverage proven per ruleCODE

The step that matters most is the rule sweep: the system must return a verdict on every rule in the firm's rulebook — a finding anchored to the exact sentence, or an explicit "checked, clean" with a reason. The count is enforced by code. Coverage is not hoped for; it is a file (rule_coverage.json) delivered next to every review.

Why not just paste the report into a chatbot?

Because a chat gives you a sample; this gives you a guarantee. Five differences, each testable: (1) a chat produces different findings every run — this checks all rules, every run, provably, and measures its own run-to-run consistency (see the box below). (2) A chat knows BREEAM; it doesn't know Sureal — the rulebook is the firm's own judgment, found in no model's training data. When we measured it, a generic review deployed zero of the firm's calibrated rules; this system deploys all of them. (3) A chat outputs text to copy-paste; this writes anchored Word comments into the client's own file. (4) A chat forgets each session; here, every reviewer reply becomes candidate training for next month's rulebook. (5) A chat changes behaviour silently when the vendor updates; this system is versioned — behaviour changes arrive as deliberate, tested releases you can diff. Full measured evidence: Addendum A.

And a chat “project” with the rulebook attached?

A fair upgrade to try — load the rulebook into a persistent project (Claude Projects and similar features), and every conversation starts with the firm's knowledge already in context. It is where our own first prototype started, and it genuinely narrows difference (2). But it leaves the other four untouched — and it only half-solves the one it addresses. Rules sitting in the context window are material the model may draw on, not checks it must perform: nothing enforces a verdict on every rule, nothing writes a coverage ledger, and no two runs apply the same subset. You have built a better-informed sample — still not a guarantee. Delivery is still copy-paste (3); reviewer replies still evaporate when the tab closes (4); and the attached knowledge file now evolves by hand-edit — unversioned, unapproved, unmeasured — which is silent drift (5) moved in-house rather than eliminated. The difference between a project and this system is the difference between handing an intern the manual and running an audit: the same information is in the room, with an entirely different guarantee about what was done with it.

Measured, August 2026. We ran 14 real client reports — every calibrated credit plus two uncalibrated ones — through the system twice, end to end. All 28 runs completed with every audit delivered. 89% of individual rule verdicts were identical across the two runs — and the system names each rule that wavered, feeding the next calibration round. A chat can't tell you its own consistency; this system measures it on every release.

Coming next (v1.3): we identified the single component responsible for most of the remaining variance — the AI-based consolidation step — and are replacing it with accountable code that keeps an auditable ledger of every finding. Measured target: pushing verdict stability beyond 90%, with the before/after numbers published in the release notes. Improvements arrive as versioned, measured releases — never as silent drift.

10 · This is a V1 — where it goes from here

What you are using today is the first rung of a ladder. The destination is not "AI instead of experts" — it is a steadily rising division of labour in which the human never leaves the loop; the loop moves up.

V1 — the tireless junior you are here
The system drafts the review; every calibrated rule is swept and proven. Experts still author the final judgment. Value: senior hours stop being spent on mechanical and rulebook checks. Reviewer time ≈ −40 %.
V2 — the reliable colleague
Months of reply-verdicts and monthly calibration have tuned the rulebook to the team. Drafts need visibly less correction; the coverage ledger ships with every client review as a rigour statement. Requires: sustained calibration discipline (§6). Reviewer time ≈ −60 %.
V3 — validation-ready
The report review arrives practically flawless in Sureal's own voice — house terminology, house structure, house judgment — because the style itself is learned from accumulated verdicts. The expert's role shifts from author to approver: read, decide, sign. Requires: the evaluation harness proving per-rule precision, so "practically flawless" is a measured claim, not a feeling. Reviewer time ≈ −80 %.
V4 — the standard
New staff are trained by the system's rulebook rather than the reverse. The firm's expertise is an institutional asset — versioned, auditable, teachable — not tacit knowledge in a few senior heads.

Each rung is earned by data, not declared by software: V2 by calibration months, V3 by measured precision. The calculator above has a stage toggle — the business case for climbing is yours to inspect.

Beyond the ladder — the iteration engine

The ladder describes the review getting better. The bigger opportunity is the rest of the value chain. Look at the life of a credit report today:

Evidence & figuresmaps · screenshots · annotationsHUMAN
Authoringsomeone types every reportHUMAN

Correctionreviewer + AI commentsHUMAN + AI
Validation & deliverysign and sendHUMAN

Only one step is automated so far. Every [HUMAN] box is a candidate for the same treatment — and because the system's knowledge lives in a repository agents can read, the system can propose these expansions itself. A concrete example: for transport credits, a person today takes map screenshots and hand-annotates transit stops, walking distances and amenities. An agent that has read this project can notice that, propose it, and build a prototype that generates those figures from a project address — evaluated against the figures in historical reports, approved by a human, and only then added to the pipeline. Analyse → propose → prototype → measure → approve → graduate: the same gated loop that governs the rulebook, applied to the software's own scope.

That is the story this project tells: not a tool that was built once, but a system that keeps finding its own next improvement — with Sureal's experts holding the approval pen at every step.

What it doesn't do (yet)

  • Uncalibrated credits run with an empty rulebook. The mechanical checks and the audit wave still run and still catch real issues — but there are no firm-specific rules to sweep until that credit has been calibrated (§6). The review says so, plainly.
  • V7 packs are scaffolds. They run, but they wait for real V7 review documents before they can learn anything worth applying.
  • Consistency is measured and improving — not absolute. 89% of rule verdicts identical across duplicate runs today, beyond 90% targeted in v1.3, with the numbers published each release. No review process, human or machine, is perfectly repeatable; this one is the only one that tells you its number.
  • It reviews; it doesn't author. The judgment, the signature, and the client relationship stay with your experts. That is the design, not a limitation.
